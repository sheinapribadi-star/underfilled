Underfilled

Most trial risk tools ask whether the drug will work. This one asks a duller question that kills more studies: will anyone sign up? It reads every Phase 2 and Phase 3 interventional trial on ClinicalTrials.gov (Phase 1/2 and 2/3 included) that started from 2010 to 2019 and is now marked completed or terminated, and shows which design choices travel with a stop for low accrual.

Section A · the finding

1,960 trials stopped because they could not enroll.

That is 31% of the 6,357 terminated trials in the cohort, more than safety and efficacy stops put together. Across all 38,287 trials, about 1 in 20 ended this way.

        Section C · what the registry shows

        Section D · model card

        How much to trust the number

        A regularized logistic regression on 43 design fields a sponsor knows before the first patient. To test it honestly, it was trained on trials that started 2010 to 2016 and scored on trials that started 2017 to 2019, which it never saw. The live estimate above uses a refit on all years.

        Known limits

        • Registry text is self-reported. Sponsors write whyStopped however they like, and 622 terminated trials left it blank.
        • Site counts are the final registered list. A study that stopped early may never have added its later sites, which flatters the "more sites" signal.
        • Withdrawn and unknown-status trials are not in the cohort. Trials that never enrolled anyone are often marked withdrawn, so the true rate of enrollment failure is higher than shown.
        • No enrollment target. Terminated trials report actual enrollment, not the plan, so target size could not be used fairly.
        • COVID-19 stops are their own bucket, even when the text also mentions slow recruitment.

        Section E · why this exists

        Where the idea came from

        At Cal Hacks 12.0 in October 2025, most of the projects that won Regeneron’s prizes went after clinical trials. TrialScope AI took the grand prize by turning protocol PDFs into structured designs and benchmarking them against historical studies. trialmatch.ai matched patients to trials, and TrialFlow attacked visit scheduling and dropout.

        Each of them is a big build with an AI layer. Underfilled is the small, checkable piece underneath: the base rates. Before anyone writes a matching agent, a study team should know how often designs like theirs ran dry, and which knobs moved with it.
